# Adaptive-Resource-Allocation-Models
Computational models intended as conceptual tools meant to demonstrate the potential of computational approaches to support climate responsive public health planning


# Climate Driven Malaria Expansion and Resource Allocation: A Spatial Reinforcement Learning Model

I developed this project to explore how climate driven changes in temperature and environmental conditions could affect malaria transmission and how limited vaccination resources could be allocated across a spatially heterogeneous population.
The model combines temperature-dependent mosquito and parasite dynamics, seasonal variation, human mobility and disease importation, flooding/water availability, healthcare capacity, partial disease observation, and vaccination decisions. I implemented a SARSA reinforcement-learning agent to investigate how intervention strategies change as climate conditions change.
I also extended the model to include socioeconomic heterogeneity by allowing wealth to affect vaccine uptake and healthcare capacity.
Current limitations: The model is a synthetic simulation rather than a calibrated predictive epidemiological model. Several parameters are stylized or require empirical calibration, and the spatial geography is intentionally simplified. My current work is focused on sensitivity analysis, improving the mobility and transmission formulations, and testing how different climate and socioeconomic scenarios affect the results.
